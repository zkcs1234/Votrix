# VOTRIX System Workflow

In organizer-facing language, a **Participant Information Form** can be called a **Participant Registration Form**. It is the form used to collect the details required from people who join an event.

VOTRIX is a secure, web-based platform for managing elections, competitions, and polling activities through role-based access. The system begins with the administrator, who oversees platform operations, creates and manages organizer accounts, monitors registered organizations and events, maintains system settings, reviews audit records, and observes platform-level statistics. Organizers access their assigned organization workspace to create and configure events, provide event details and branding, define the rules and settings, build participant information forms, and manage the users involved in each activity. For elections, organizers create positions, register candidates, invite or import voters, activate voting, and review turnout and voting results; for competitions, organizers register contestants, create categories and rounds, define judging criteria and scoring weights, invite judges, assign judges to specific events or rounds, control live sessions, and view rankings, analytics, and reports; and for polling, organizers create surveys using question types such as single choice, multiple selection, yes/no, rating, Likert scale, open text, and ranking, while configuring options such as anonymous responses, multiple submissions, and expiration dates. Judges, who are event-scoped users with competition judging access, log in to review the contestants assigned to them and submit scores according to the criteria and rounds configured by the organizer. Students, voters, respondents, and other invited participants use their accounts to access only the events in which they are enrolled: voters complete election ballots, respondents answer polling or survey questions, and students or other designated participants may provide information, cast votes, submit responses, or participate in competition-related activities depending on the event role assigned to them. The platform authenticates users, applies role and event-participant permissions, requires password changes when applicable, protects requests through secure token and CSRF controls, prevents unauthorized or duplicate submissions, sends invitations and notifications through email, stores event and participation records in the database, and provides dashboards, analytics, rankings, and reports for monitoring outcomes. This end-to-end workflow provides the basis for pilot testing the system's usability, functionality, security, reliability, role separation, event participation, data recording, and reporting performance from the administrator's initial setup through the organizer's management tasks and the final activities of judges, students, voters, respondents, and other users.
