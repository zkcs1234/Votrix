Chapter I
INTRODUCTION
Background of the Study
Online polling and voting systems play a critical role in supporting democratic participation, organizational governance, and competitive evaluation in academic institutions. In many colleges and universities, the conduct of elections, student polls, and competition judging is still managed through manual or paper-based processes, which frequently result in delays in tallying, difficulties in maintaining the integrity of results, and vulnerabilities to human error and manipulation. These challenges undermine the credibility and efficiency of institutional decision-making processes, particularly in environments where accuracy, fairness, and transparency are paramount.
According to the International Institute for Democracy and Electoral Assistance (IDEA, 2021), the adoption of digital voting and polling technologies in institutional settings significantly enhances the integrity and efficiency of electoral and evaluative processes. These systems leverage web-based platforms, structured ballot management, and real-time result computation to improve the accuracy of vote collection, reduce administrative bottlenecks, and strengthen the overall security of the voting process. By transitioning from manual to digital frameworks, institutions can minimize common vulnerabilities such as ballot tampering, double voting, and unauthorized access to results.
Presently, at academic institutions such as Bohol Island State University - 
Calape
, voting and competition scoring are conducted manually through physical 
balloting, handwritten tally sheets, and in-person evaluation by designated judges or officers. Voter and candidate records are often managed through printed forms or unstructured digital files, making it difficult to enforce eligibility rules, monitor participation, and accurately and promptly consolidate competition scores. The absence of a unified digital platform further exposes these processes to inconsistencies, delays, and disputes over results.
In response to these institutional challenges, the system VOTRIX is designed to centralize voter registration, enforce eligibility and participation rules, and automate the processing of votes and competition scores. It introduces a structured digital workflow that encompasses administrative management, candidate or entry registration, voter authentication, secure ballot submission, and automated result computation and scoring. Furthermore, the system strengthens process integrity by implementing duplicate-vote-prevention mechanisms and role-based access controls for judges, administrators, and voters. Through these features, VOTRIX aims to improve the efficiency, accuracy, and transparency of institutional polling, elections, and competition evaluation at BISU 
Calape
.
Objectives of the Study
The main objective was to design and implement a system that conducts polls, elections, and competition judging faster, more transparently, and more organized through automation and proper enforcement of voting rules and eligibility requirements.
Specifically, it was guided by the following specific objectives:
To analyze the existing process of conducting polls, elections, and competition scoring in BISU 
Calape
, including voter registration, candidate entry, ballot management, and result tabulation and judging procedures.
To develop a web-based online poll and voting system that streamlines voter and candidate registration, voter authentication, ballot submission, result computation, and competition score tabulation, while incorporating controls such as duplicate vote prevention and role-based access management.
To evaluate the system in terms of usability and system acceptability, as well as efficiency, transparency, and accuracy in the conduct and monitoring of polls, elections, and competition scoring activities.
To create a comprehensive user manual that guides users in effectively operating and navigating the web-based online poll and voting system with integrated competition scoring.
Significance of the Study
This study provides significant benefits to key stakeholders involved in implementing and managing event scoring and judging systems at Bohol Island State University - 
Calape
. Administrative staff, event coordinators, and judges benefit from improved operational efficiency through the automation of essential processes, including participant registration, judge credential management, scoring sheet generation, and real-time tabulation. Reducing manual procedures minimizes errors, improves scoring consistency, and accelerates result processing across various events, including pageants, dance competitions, intramurals, and 
other institutional activities. Centralized data management and automated reporting also enhance transparency, accountability, and informed decision-making.
Participants and students benefit from a more accessible, organized, and transparent competition process. The system promotes fairness through standardized judging criteria and reduces administrative challenges associated with manual scoring methods. Real-time score tracking and result visibility further improve participant confidence and trust in the integrity of the judging process.
Future researchers may use this study as a reference in developing web-based event management and scoring systems. The study provides a scalable framework incorporating multi-event support, automated tabulation, dynamic scoring mechanisms, and real-time result aggregation. It also offers insights into system design, security considerations, and evaluation approaches that may support future innovations and adaptations in educational institutions and similar competitive environments.
Scope and Limitation of the Study
The study focused on developing a web-based polling and voting system, VOTRIX, designed for integrated competition scoring and electoral management at Bohol Island State University–
Calape
. The system covered the entire workflow of the polling and voting process, starting from voter registration through result publication. It included modules for voter authentication, eligibility verification, poll creation and management, real-time vote casting, automated competition scoring algorithms, vote counting, judge assignment, and comprehensive result 
compilation and report generation. The system was designed to ensure that only verified and eligible voters could participate in elections and competitions, with strict enforcement of a one-vote-per-person rule. Each registered voter was limited to a single vote per designated election or competition. In addition, the system integrated duplicate-vote prevention mechanisms, automated ballot-number generation, real-time vote tracking (Pending Votes, Votes Submitted, and Results Processed), competition-scoring modules for weighted evaluation and candidate ranking, and report-generation features to support transparency and proper monitoring of election administration within the university organization.
The study was limited to a web-based platform. It did not include a mobile application version or offline voting functionality; therefore, system access depended on internet connectivity and device compatibility. The system also relied on accurate data entry and authentication procedures performed by authorized personnel, such as administrators, organizers, election officers, and designated staff, and it did not automatically validate information from external national identification systems. Competition scoring was limited to arithmetic mean, weighted mean with user-defined weights, percentile ranking, ordinal ranking, and Likert scale aggregation methods, and did not include advanced statistical methods beyond these institutional approaches. All voting processes were strictly limited to poll management, vote recording, automated scoring, and results tracking within the VOTRIX environment. Formal declaration of results, official certification, and post-election audit procedures remained the responsibility of the designated election commission and university administration outside the system. 
Furthermore, the scope of the study was limited to system testing, evaluation, and implementation in a controlled environment at BISU 
Calape
. It did not cover nationwide deployment, integration with the Commission on Elections (COMELEC) systems, or real-time networked voting across multiple geographic locations.

REVIEW OF RELATED LITERATURE
Related Literature
In recent years, the rapid advancement of web-based and digital technologies has profoundly transformed how organizations, institutions, and communities conduct structured evaluative processes, ranging from organizational elections and community opinion polling to judge-scored competitive events such as pageants, dance competitions, talent shows, and other performance-based contests. Traditional approaches that rely on paper ballots, manual score tallying, and handwritten tally sheets have proven increasingly inadequate in addressing the demands of accuracy, transparency, and efficiency that modern events require. This digital transformation reflects a broader movement to leverage information technology to support credible, real-time, and participatory decision-making across all scored and voted activities.
Building on this, Darmawan (2021), in his peer-reviewed work published in the Asian Journal of Comparative Politics, conducted a semi-systematic review of 78 studies on electronic voting adoption across multiple countries from 2005 to 2020. The study established that electronic voting platforms are increasingly recognized as tools that reduce the costs and administrative burden of traditional voting processes while improving accessibility, participation rates, and the reliability of results. Darmawan further argued that the theoretical framing of e-voting has evolved beyond purely electoral applications, encompassing any structured decision-making process in which participants cast choices that must 
be accurately counted and reported. This conceptual expansion is directly applicable to VOTRIX, which serves not only organizational elections and public polls but also competitive events that rely on public or panel-based voting to generate results.
From a broader technological perspective, Jafar, Aziz, and Shukur (2021), in their open-access article published in MDPI Sensors, provided a comprehensive review of electronic voting systems and the principles that underpin their integrity. Their review established that any credible digital voting or evaluation system must satisfy four core properties: legitimacy, accuracy, safety, and convenience. The authors emphasized that these properties are not exclusive to electoral systems but extend to any digital platform designed to collect, process, and report evaluative inputs from multiple participants. In the context of competitive events such as beauty pageants or dance competitions, where judge inputs determine rankings and outcomes, the same standards of system integrity, tamper resistance, verifiable data processing, and transparent result generation must be upheld. Despite the growing theoretical consensus on these requirements, many existing platforms continue to fall short of meeting all four properties simultaneously, particularly in non-electoral competitive settings.
In relation to the theoretical foundations of digital polling and public participation platforms, the United Nations Development 
Programme
 (UNDP, 2021) emphasized in its report on digital governance that inclusive and transparent digital participation systems must be designed with multi-stakeholder functionality accommodating the distinct roles of administrators, voters or judges, and 
participants or candidates. The report further highlighted that the credibility of any digital decision-making process, whether a public opinion poll or a scored competition, is directly dependent on the system's capacity to enforce structured access controls, generate auditable result trails, and present outcomes in real time. These governance-oriented design principles form the conceptual foundation of VOTRIX, which supports role-differentiated access for event administrators, voters, and judges across multiple event types within a unified web-based environment.
The Commission on Elections (COMELEC) of the Philippines (2022) has made significant strides in advancing the country's digital evaluation infrastructure by continuing to modernize automated election systems and promoting digital participation in both national and local governance processes. These institutional developments underscore the applicability of digital voting concepts to the Philippine context and demonstrate that the principles governing electronic result processing, including duplicate prevention, authenticated access, and real-time tabulation, are both legally recognized and operationally viable in local settings. In relation to VOTRIX, this institutional precedent underscores the need for a web-based platform that applies the same rigorous data management standards to formal elections, institutional competitions, school-based events, and organizational activities that require systematic scoring and the publication of verifiable results.
In the specific context of multi-criteria evaluation and competition scoring theory, Cai and Xiang (2024), in their study titled "Multifactor and Multidimensional 
Data Quality Analysis of Judge Scoring in Diving Competition," published in Frontiers in Psychology, examined the theoretical and methodological challenges associated with judge-based scoring in competitive events. The study demonstrated that competitions involving multiple judges scoring participants across several criteria are inherently prone to scoring inconsistencies, bias, and data-quality issues when managed manually or with fragmented tools. The authors proposed that structured digital evaluation systems capable of aggregating multi-judge scores, applying weighted criteria, and computing statistically reliable rankings are essential to ensuring the fairness and credibility of competitive results across domains such as sports, performing arts, and institutional pageantry.
However, despite the recognized value of digital systems in improving evaluation processes, the study identified persistent limitations in their practical application to multi-event and multi-criteria scenarios. These include the absence of unified platforms capable of managing both popular-vote components and judge-scored segments, the lack of configurable scoring rubrics adaptable to different competition types such as pageants, dance, or talent events, and insufficient real-time result visualization tools that allow organizers and audiences to monitor competition standings as they develop. These challenges were largely attributed to the narrow design scope of most existing evaluation tools, which address either voting or scoring in isolation and rarely integrate both functions into a cohesive, end-to-end event management system. Addressing these limitations provides the primary justification for developing VOTRIX, which consolidates online polling, organizational voting, multi-criteria judge-based scoring, and 
automated rank computation into a single, secure, and user-accessible web-based platform designed to serve the full spectrum of evaluative and competitive event management needs.

Related Studies
Cornelio et al. (2025) developed the MEMU Tabulation System, a digital platform for automating cultural contest scoring, email notifications, and e-certificate generation. Evaluated using ISO 25010, the system demonstrated excellent reliability and functionality. However, it was limited exclusively to event scoring and did not include any online voting, polling, or multi-role election management features. VOTRIX addresses these limitations by integrating online polling, secure electronic voting, and competition scoring into a unified web-based platform.
Ramizares
 and 
Ramizares
 (2025) introduced an Adaptive Multi-Event Tabulation System that automated score computation across multiple competition types using customizable event modules and responsive judge interfaces. The system addressed tabulation efficiency but did not incorporate secure online voting, public polling, or role-based participant authentication. VOTRIX addresses this gap through its combined voting, polling, and role-based management functionalities for elections and competitions.
Reyes et al. (2025) developed 
VoSys
, a hybrid online voting platform for academic department elections. 
VoSys
 employed biometric authentication and digital ballot processing to improve election security and efficiency. The system was specialized for electoral management and lacked capabilities for competition scoring, event tabulation, or multi-event administration. VOTRIX addresses these shortcomings by integrating election management with competition scoring and automated tabulation features.
Afable and 
Quiloña
 (2020) designed a Multi-User Automated Pageant Tabulation System for local government competitions, demonstrating faster and more accurate results than manual computation. The platform was limited to pageant judging and lacked online voting, public polling, and secure user authentication. Such limitations are overcome in VOTRIX through secure online elections, polling functionalities, and multi-role administrative access.
Atanacio et al. (2024) evaluated 
AutoBeaut
, an automated judging system for beauty pageants used across five years of institutional events. Rated "Very Acceptable" under ISO 25010, 
AutoBeaut
 demonstrated long-term operational reliability. However, the system functioned solely as a standalone judging application and did not offer voting, polling, or integrated election management. VOTRIX addresses this deficiency through its comprehensive platform that combines voting, polling, and competition management capabilities.
Alves and Pinto (2023) explored a Blockchain-Assisted Electronic Voting System for remote academic council meetings, emphasizing vote integrity through blockchain transparency. The system addressed secure digital voting but was limited to that domain and did not include competition scoring, polling, or multi-category event management. The proposed VOTRIX system addresses these limitations by unifying elections, polling, and competition scoring within a single platform.
Hochbaum and Moreno-Centeno (2022) proposed a mathematical aggregation framework combining ordinal and cardinal evaluations for academic paper competitions. While significant as a conceptual contribution to scoring 
fairness, the framework was purely theoretical and did not involve the development of a deployable web-based system with voting, polling, or real-time tabulation features. VOTRIX fills this gap by providing a practical web-based implementation with integrated voting, polling, and real-time tabulation functionalities.
Salunke et al. (2025) developed the NextGen Voting System, which utilizes unique student identifiers and encrypted authentication to strengthen transparency in academic elections and prevent duplicate voting. The system was effective for its electoral purpose, but did not include competition scoring, automated ranking, or multi-event management functionality. These limitations are addressed in VOTRIX through integrated competition scoring, automated ranking, and centralized event administration features.
The reviewed literature indicates a clear limitation in existing systems and studies: no single platform combines online polling, secure electronic voting, and automated competition scoring within a unified, web-based, multi-role environment. Each reviewed study addresses only one or two of these functional components separately. Systems focused on competition tabulation, such as MEMU, Adaptive Tabulation, and 
AutoBeaut
, provide reliable scoring mechanisms but do not incorporate electoral processes or audience participation features. Systems focused on electronic voting, such as 
VoSys
, the Blockchain Vote System, and the NextGen Voting System, offer secure ballot management but do not extend to competition judging, weighted scoring, or event tabulation. Theoretical frameworks, such as the aggregation methodology by Dorit S. Hochbaum and Esteban Moreno-Centeno (2022), provide important conceptual 
contributions to scoring fairness but do not yield deployable systems that integrate voting and polling functionality.
Furthermore, no reviewed study presented a system that simultaneously serves as an election management tool, a public polling platform, and a competition scoring engine within a single institutional environment. This multi-purpose integration is increasingly needed in educational institutions and organizations where school elections, pageants, and other scored events are conducted in proximity or even concurrently. Managing these events across separate, disconnected systems introduces administrative redundancy, data inconsistency, and limited transparency.
The proposed Online Poll and Voting System with Integrated Competition Scoring directly addresses this gap by developing a unified web-based platform that combines secure, role-based electronic voting, real-time competition scoring with weighted criteria, and public polling. This approach eliminates the limitations of single-purpose platforms, reduces administrative overhead, and provides a transparent, accessible, and scalable solution for both electoral and competition management needs in academic and organizational contexts.

Requirement Analysis
The requirement analysis identified the scope, stakeholders, and the functional, non-functional, technical, and data requirements that guided the development of the VOTRIX system.
1. Define the Scope
VOTRIX is a web-based platform that manages online elections, polling or surveys, and competition scoring for Bohol Island State University – 
Calape
. Its scope includes account and event management, participant invitation, secure ballot and score submission, duplicate-vote prevention, and the generation of analytics and reports. Hardware procurement and offline balloting are outside the scope of the system.
2. Identify Stakeholders
The primary stakeholders are the administrator, who manages organizer accounts and oversees the platform; the organizer, who creates and manages events; and the voter, who participates in an event as an election voter, a 
competition judge, or a polling respondent, depending on the assigned participant type.
3. Gather Functional Requirements
The system must allow secure authentication and role-based access; creation and configuration of election, competition, and polling events; uploading of branding assets; invitation of participants; submission of votes, scores, or responses; prevention of duplicate submissions; and the generation of turnout, ranking, and result analytics.
4. Gather Non-Functional Requirements
The system must be secure, applying password hashing, token-based authentication, HTTP-only cookies, CSRF protection, role-based route protection, and rate limiting. It must also be usable, reliable, maintainable, and responsive across supported devices.
5. Identify Technical Requirements
The front end is built with React, Vite, and Tailwind CSS, with 
Zustand
, React Router, Axios, React Hook Form, Zod, and Framer Motion. The back end uses Node.js and Express, with JSON Web Tokens and 
bcrypt
 for authentication. Data is stored in a PostgreSQL database hosted on 
Supabase
, while 
Cloudinary
 handles media storage and Resend handles email delivery. A WebSocket channel provides real-time updates for live rankings, dashboard statistics, and live-session control.
6. Identify Data Requirements
The system maintains data for users, organizations, events, event participants, ballots and votes, poll questions and answers, contestants and candidates, criteria and scores, and the computed analytics. Universally unique identifiers (UUIDs) are used as primary keys, and the schema is managed through numbered SQL migrations.
7. Requirements in Paragraph Form
Taken together, the requirements describe a secure, role-based platform in which an administrator manages organizers, an organizer configures and operates election, competition, or polling events, and a voter participates according to the assigned participant type. The system enforces eligibility and event rules, prevents duplicate submissions, stores all records in a relational PostgreSQL database, and produces analytics for organizers and administrators, all delivered through a React front end communicating with an Express REST API over secure, cookie-based sessions.
System Architecture
VOTRIX follows a three-tier client–server architecture with an auxiliary real-time channel. The client tier is a React single-page application that renders the administrator, organizer, and voter/judge/respondent interfaces and communicates with the server exclusively over HTTPS REST calls carrying HTTP-only session cookies. The application tier is a Node.js and Express REST API that enforces authentication and authorization through a middleware chain — authenticate, authorize by role, 
requireActiveAccount
, 
requirePasswordChanged
, 
requireProfileComplete
, and 
requireEventParticipant
 — before delegating to a service layer that holds the business logic for the election, competition, and polling modules, including the shared scoring engine. The data tier is a PostgreSQL database hosted on 
Supabase
, accessed through service functions and, for the integrity-critical vote and poll submissions, through PL/
pgSQL
 stored procedures that make each submission a single atomic transaction. Two external services complete the architecture: 
Cloudinary
 for image storage (event banners, candidate and contestant photos, organization logos) and 
Resend
 for transactional email (organizer credentials, voter and judge invitations, password resets, and notifications). A WebSocket channel pushes live updates — vote tallies, competition rankings, live-session state, and dashboard statistics — to connected clients without polling. 1. Design Specification
The presentation layer is written in React with Vite and Tailwind CSS and uses 
Zustand

Competition Scoring Algorithm
Because “integrated competition scoring” is a central claim of VOTRIX, this section states the exact weighted formula used by the scoring engine (backend/
src
/modules/scoring-engine.js). The engine composes each contestant’s final score bottom-up from the individual judge cells to the event total, with normalization at each level so scales, weights, and minor criteria on different bases can be combined without a single component dominating.
1. Score Scales and Calculation Methods
The score scale (
scoreType
) is one of range_1_10 (1–10), range_1_100 (1–100, the default), decimal (0–10), or 
custom_range
 with the organizer’s own minimum and maximum. Bounds are validated at submission by 
isScoreInBounds
 using the event scale as the single source of truth. The calculation method (
calculationMethod
) determines how the set of judge scores for one cell is reduced 
to a single number: average, 
weighted_average
 (the default), sum, 
highest_score
, or 
lowest_removal
 (drops the configured lowest and highest scores, then averages the rest). The number of decimal places used for the final and intermediate values is set by 
decimalPlaces
 (0–6, default 2).
2. Bottom-Up Computation
Let J be the set of judges who scored a given cell. The engine composes the final score from the bottom up in four stages. First, each minor criterion is reduced across judges (default: average) and normalized to a percentage of its own maximum so minors on different scales can be combined: 
minorPercent
 = 
( 
reduce
_J
(score) / 
minorMax
 )
 × 100. Second, each criterion is aggregated: with minors, the criterion value is the plain average of its minors’ percents (minors carry equal weight and have no individual percentage); without minors, the criterion value is simply 
reduce_J
(score). Third, each round’s value is computed as a weighted average across the round’s criteria, using each criterion’s percentage divided by the sum of criterion percentages in that round: 
roundValue
 = 
Σ_over_round_criteria
 [ 
criterionValue
 × 
( 
criterion
.percentage
 / Σ 
criterion.percentage
_in_
round
 )
 ]. Fourth, when there are no categories, the final score is the sum over rounds of the round value multiplied by the round weight and a scale factor equal to 100 divided by the sum of round weights (in the legacy, non-scoped path, so weights that do not total 100 cannot inflate the result) or 1 in scoped mode. When categories are used, each category value is the sum over its rounds of 
roundValue
 × (
round.weight
 / 100), and the final score is the sum over categories of 
categoryValue
 × (
category.weight
 / 100), plus any event-wide rounds.
3. Ranking and Tie Handling
Contestants are sorted by final score in descending order and assigned standard competition ranks (“1224”): tied contestants share the same rank and the next distinct score resumes at its ordinal position. An optional deterministic tie-breaker (
tieBreaker
 = 
highest_criterion
) breaks ties by the contestant’s single highest per-criterion average. All numeric values are rounded to the configured decimal places. The generic “criterion × weight, then round × weight, then overall” description that appears in preliminary briefs is only approximately correct for the weighted-average path; the verified engine additionally performs minor-criterion percentage normalization, per-round criterion normalization in scoped mode, legacy round-weight normalization, category weighting, four alternate calculation methods, and 1224 tie-handling.
4. Enforced and Reported Validations
The engine enforces the following at submission time: ownership (
assertOrganizerOwnsEvent
), criterion percentage in the range 0–100 and category and round weight in the range 0–100 (database CHECK constraints), judge assignment scope belonging to the same event (scope guard, migration 063), a complete scoring grid (every contestant × criteria cell present, no duplicates), scores inside the event score scale, and scores inside the judge’s assigned scope. It reports—but does not hard-block—a criterion or round weight total that does not equal 100 percent; the engine normalizes these and surfaces the totals as feedback so the organizer can correct them. An explicit pre-scoring gate that blocks on “no judge assigned” or “no contestant” for competitions 
(analogous to the election publish minimum-content gate) is not confirmed in the current code and can be added if required.

Research Instrument
The study used a structured survey questionnaire as its main research instrument. The questionnaire was adapted from the ISO/IEC 25010 software quality model and standard usability instruments, and it measured the respondents’ assessment of the system in terms of functional suitability, performance efficiency, usability, reliability, security, and overall acceptability. Each item was rated using a four-point Likert scale, and the responses were interpreted using the weighted mean and the corresponding scale ranges shown in Table 3.
Research Environment and Respondents
The study was conducted at Bohol Island State University – 
Calape
 Campus, where polls, elections, and competitions are regularly held. The respondents were selected through purposive sampling and included students, faculty or staff involved in conducting elections and competitions, and system administrators who evaluated the functionality and usability of the system. [The specific number of respondents may be finalized here.]
Data Gathering Procedure
After securing the necessary permissions, the researchers oriented the respondents on the purpose of the study and the use of the system. The 
respondents then used the VOTRIX system to perform representative tasks (logging in, participating in a test election or competition or poll, and reviewing results) and afterward answered the evaluation questionnaire. The accomplished questionnaires were retrieved, tallied, and analyzed using the appropriate statistical treatment.

Definition of Terms
Administrator. 
the highest-level user who manages user accounts (organizers, voters, and judges), the participant taxonomy, and platform policy. Administrators authenticate with an email and password (the same unified login used by organizers and voters) and cannot create or run events themselves.
Organizer. 
the user who creates and manages election, competition, or polling events. Organizer accounts are created by the administrator and require the four onboarding fields (organization name, organization type, organizer name, position) before any module route is unlocked.
Voter (account level). 
the participant-level user who casts a vote, scores a competition, or answers a poll, depending on the assigned participant type. A single voter account can hold different participant types across different events.
Participant Type. 
the event-specific role of a voter, namely ELECTION_VOTER, COMPETITION_JUDGE, or POLLING_RESPONDENT. The participant type is stored per event in 
event_participants
 and is enforced at request time by the 
requireEventParticipant
 middleware.
Ballot. 
the digital form through which a voter submits a vote in an election event. The ballot carries a single-use voting nonce to prevent replay.
Voting Nonce. 
a single-use token minted into 
event_participants
 when a voter opens a ballot; the server rejects a submitted ballot whose nonce does not match the current stored value.
Duplicate-Vote Prevention. 
the mechanism that ensures a participant can submit only one valid response per event. In VOTRIX it is enforced twice: by the atomic 
has_voted
 claim inside the 
cast_election_ballot
 RPC and by the unique constraint on 
election_votes
.
Role-Based Access Control. 
the security mechanism that restricts access to features and data based on the user’s role and, for event routes, the user’s participant type.
Scoring Engine. 
the shared module (scoring-engine.js) that computes weighted rankings from raw judge scores using the event’s scoring configuration.
Live Competition Session. 
a single active per-event session in which the organizer controls the current round, criteria, and contestant, and judges submit per-contestant scores that are pushed over the WebSocket in real time.
Standard Competition Ranking (“1224”). 
the ranking scheme in which tied contestants share the same rank and the next distinct score resumes at its ordinal position.
Schedule-Driven Lifecycle. 
the model in which event openness is derived from 
start_date
 and 
end_date
 plus the module’s enabled flag, rather than from a manual open / close toggle.
Draft. 
an autosaved in-progress event of one module (election, competition, or polling) per organizer, stored in 
event_drafts
 and cleared when the event is created.
Audit Log. 
the append-only record (
audit_logs
) of security- and event-relevant actions, written by 
recordAudit
 and 
recordEventActivity
. Vote records are audited as counts only, preserving secret-ballot confidentiality.
